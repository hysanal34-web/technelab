Choosing an external drama or musical partner for a school in Istanbul comes down to five questions: who will be in the room with your students, what qualifies them, where and when they will rehearse, who holds the rights to what goes on stage, and how images of students will be handled. A provider who answers these clearly, in writing, before the first session is a provider you can plan a year with.

This guide is for heads, arts coordinators and English departments. It is not legal advice; where Turkish regulation is involved, we say what we could and could not verify.

## Why this decision is harder than it looks

Most drama providers in Istanbul are built for families, not for schools. In our own market research this autumn, we found very few providers with a page written for schools at all, and almost none that set out format, group size, space requirements or a consent policy. European touring companies that visit schools often do publish this kind of detail, but the ones we looked at did not list Turkey.

So schools often learn how a provider works after signing. The questions below move that conversation earlier.

## 1. Safeguarding: who is in the room?

- **Named people.** Ask exactly who will lead each session and who may substitute.
- **Checks and policy.** Ask what background checks the provider's staff have and whether the provider has its own written safeguarding policy. Then ask how it fits with yours. We could not find a public Turkish school procurement standard that sets out these requirements, so your own school policy is the reference point.
- **Ratio and supervision.** Will a member of school staff be present? Agree on it in writing.
- **Physical work.** Drama involves touch, lifts and close contact. Ask how the provider handles consent for physical exercises and what happens when a student opts out.

## 2. Staff qualifications: what do they actually know?

"Experienced drama teacher" covers a lot of ground. Ask for biographies of the people who will actually work with your students.

Look for three things:

- **Stage practice.** Have they directed, performed or choreographed for an audience?
- **Work with young people.** Have they led groups of the age you are booking for?
- **Language.** For English-medium work, can they run a whole session in English?

Ask about training directly: where the trainers studied and what they have staged. Ours are conservatory-trained actors and trainers.

If you are an IB or Cambridge school, ask whether the provider understands the assessment you are working toward. The IB DP Theatre course includes a production proposal, a research presentation, a collaborative project and, at Higher Level, a solo theatre piece (IBO). Cambridge IGCSE Drama (0411), for 2028 examinations, weights coursework at 60%, including a group devised piece (Cambridge International). A partner who knows these components can support them.

## 3. Rehearsal access: where, when, how often?

- **Space.** Will the provider work in your hall, a classroom, the gym? What do they need: floor space, a piano, a sound system, a lockable room for costumes?
- **Calendar.** Ask for a draft rehearsal schedule mapped onto your school calendar, including exam weeks and holidays. A musical that opens in May needs its music learned well before spring.
- **Technical rehearsal.** When will the first full run happen? When is the technical rehearsal with lights and sound? If both fall in the final week, push back.

## 4. Performance rights: who owns what goes on stage?

If the production uses a published play or musical, someone usually needs permission from the rights holder or their agent, even for a free school performance. Translation, cuts and adaptation may need separate permission. Ask the provider directly:

- Who applies for the rights, the school or the provider?
- Who pays, and is it included in the proposal?
- If the piece is devised or original, who owns the script afterwards?

There is a repertoire question specific to Turkey as well. An amendment to the regulation on private education institutions bans activities considered contrary to "national and cultural values", and the same news report refers to a Ministry letter from December 2023 (Memurlar.net, 2024). If a holiday-themed musical is on the table, check with your administration first.

## 5. Photo and video consent: what will happen to the images?

This has become the most sensitive question in school performance. According to a press report, in July 2026 the Ministry of National Education (MEB) instructed that student and staff photos and videos should not be published on school websites and social media, and that existing content should be removed (GZT, 2026). We could not verify whether, or how, this instruction applies to private schools.

So we cannot offer you a rule. What we can suggest is a set of questions:

- Will the provider film or photograph at all? If so, who stores it, for how long, and who sees it?
- Will the provider use any images for its own marketing? (The safest answer, for a school, is no faces.)
- What written parental consent does your school already collect, and does it cover this?

Check your school's own procedure, and if in doubt, ask your legal adviser.

## A note on permits for outside organisations

Turkey's Ministry directive on permits for social activities (in force since 31 August 2020) covers activities run in schools by public bodies, NGOs and other organisations. A partial summary we could read mentions applications through ayse.meb.gov.tr at least 45 days before the activity, written parental consent, and a ban on commercial activity and brand promotion (Lexpera summary). Whether a private school buying services such as a club instructor or production support falls under this directive, or is a separate contractual matter, we could not verify. Ask a lawyer; do not rely on a provider's assurance, including ours.

## What a good proposal looks like

A strong proposal names the people, maps the calendar, states space needs, answers the rights question and sets out a consent policy. It does not promise outcomes it cannot control. We are preparing our own school offer on these lines; you can see what we cover on our [schools page](/en/schools).

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a school ask an external drama provider first?**
Who exactly will be in the room with students, what checks they have, and how the provider's safeguarding policy fits with the school's own. Artistic questions come after these.

**Does a free school performance need performance rights?**
For published plays and musicals, usually yes. A free performance does not by itself remove the need for permission. Terms vary by work and rights holder, so settle the question before casting.

**Can the provider post photos of our students?**
Only if your school's procedure and parental consent allow it. A July 2026 press report says MEB instructed schools not to publish student images online; we could not verify how this applies to private schools. Check your school's procedure.
