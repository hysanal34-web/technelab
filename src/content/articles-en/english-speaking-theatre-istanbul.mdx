Istanbul is one of the great theatre cities of the region. It has municipal theatres with seasons of dozens of productions, a state theatre, an international festival that has run for three decades, and a dense independent scene spread between Beyoğlu and Kadıköy. Almost all of it happens in Turkish.

So if you live here, or are passing through, and you search for "English theatre Istanbul", what you mostly find is tourist listicles, an old forum thread or two, and pages that promise English subtitles "on select nights" without telling you which nights, or where.

This guide tries to do better. It is written by an independent theatre company working in English and Turkish in Pera and Kadıköy, and it sticks to what we could verify in October 2026. It is organised around three questions: where can you **watch** theatre without Turkish, where can you **join** something in English, and where can you **perform**?

One honest answer first.

## Is there theatre in English in Istanbul?

Not much, and very little of it is regular.

There is no permanent English-language theatre company with a public season that we could verify as active in 2026. The municipal City Theatres (İBB Şehir Tiyatroları) and the State Theatres perform in Turkish, and we could not find an official statement from either about English surtitles. Independent stages occasionally run a production with English surtitles; Moda Sahnesi in Kadıköy, for example, has listed a production as "Turkish with English surtitles" in the past. But these are exceptions, and you need to check each production.

That does not mean you are shut out. It means you need to know where to look, and it means the most reliable way to have theatre in English in your life here is to take part rather than to watch.

There are a lot of you, by the way. According to the Presidency of Migration Management's figures, compiled by the Göç Vakfı research foundation, Istanbul had 610,221 foreign residents with residence permits in early September 2026. That is a large audience for a scene that barely exists.

## Watching: theatre you can follow without Turkish

### The Istanbul Theatre Festival

The 30th Istanbul Theatre Festival, organised by İKSV, runs from **22 October to 3 December 2026**. It is the best moment of the year for international visitors, because it brings productions from abroad.

A crucial detail, though: on the production pages we checked, the festival's surtitles are in **Turkish**, not English. What makes some festival shows accessible is the language of the production itself:

- Some productions are partly performed in English. One of this year's productions lists its languages as Arabic, Hebrew, English and Dutch, with Turkish surtitles. Another is mainly in French, with parts in Tamil, English and French Sign Language.
- Some are largely non-verbal. One production in the programme is described as non-verbal, with British Sign Language used occasionally and English translations projected on the backdrop.
- Dance is, of course, the most accessible form of all. The festival also includes contemporary dance.

**How to use this:** go to the festival programme on the İKSV website and read each production's language line. It is usually stated clearly. If it says "in Turkish with Turkish surtitles", it is not for you. If it says non-verbal, dance, or lists English among the languages, it may well be.

### Dance, physical theatre and music theatre

Outside the festival, the most reliable way to enjoy Istanbul's stages without Turkish is to choose forms where language matters less: contemporary dance, physical theatre, opera (often surtitled), and some music theatre. Big touring productions sometimes come to the large venues. Check each listing; never assume.

### English stand-up and improv

Comedy is where English-language performance is most visible in Istanbul.

TuzBiber Stand-Up runs a dedicated "English Shows" section on its website. At the time of writing it lists an **English Open Mic on Sunday 11 October 2026 at Aylak Kadıköy**, and a John Cleese show at Lütfi Kırdar on 4 November 2026. Open mics are especially interesting if you want to move from watching to performing.

English-language improv has existed in Istanbul for years. A group called The Clap has performed improv in English; an interview with them on the expat site Yabangee dates back to 2017. We could not verify whether they are currently active, so check their social media before planning an evening around them.

### How to check whether a show is in English

Ticketing sites like Biletix and Biletinial are mostly in Turkish. A few words help:

| Turkish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| İngilizce | English |
| İngilizce üstyazı / altyazı | English surtitles / subtitles |
| Türkçe üstyazılı | with Turkish surtitles |
| Sözsüz | non-verbal |
| Dans / dans tiyatrosu | dance / dance theatre |
| Müzikal | musical (usually in Turkish) |
| Oyun dili | language of the play |

If the listing does not mention a language, the show is almost certainly in Turkish.

## Joining: doing theatre in English

This is where Istanbul gets more interesting. There are fewer places to watch English theatre than you would expect, and more places to do it than you would think.

### Drama workshops for adults

At Techne Lab, our **English Drama Lab** is a weekly drama workshop for adults, run entirely in English, in Pera (Beyoğlu) and in Kadıköy. It is built around improvisation, scene work and ensemble exercises rather than scripts to memorise. Groups are capped at twelve people. You can join for 12, 8 or 4 weeks, which matters if you are not sure how long you will be in the city. The level of English we ask for is B1 and above; the groups are open both to Turkish participants who work in English and to internationals who live here.

We run free introduction sessions every week: in Pera on Saturdays at 15:00 and in Kadıköy on Mondays at 20:00. You come, you do a session, you decide.

We are not neutral, obviously. But we are also not the only option, and you should know the others.

### English drama for children and teenagers

For families, **Speech Bubbles School of Performing Arts** runs drama in English with a strong focus on young learners, organised in Saturday terms. Their winter term ran from January to March 2026. Our own **English Drama Youth** programme is for 10 to 17-year-olds, also in Pera and Kadıköy, with weekly free introduction sessions.

### Language exchange meetups with a drama twist

Istanbul's language-exchange scene is large and mostly lives on Meetup. In October 2026, the listings include weekly language nights in Kadıköy and Üsküdar, and one English club runs a weekly evening called "Act in English" built around improv and acting games. These are informal, drop-in and social. They are a good first step if a structured workshop feels like too much.

## Performing: getting on stage in English

If you want an audience, your realistic options are:

- **Open mics.** English open mic nights for stand-up are the most accessible stage in the city.
- **End-of-term showings.** Some workshops, including some of ours, end with an informal showing for friends and family. Ask about it before you join; it changes the feel of the work.
- **Your own production.** Small independent stages in Kadıköy and Beyoğlu can be rented. If you have a group and a text, this is how many English-language productions in Istanbul have happened in the past.

## Pera or Kadıköy?

Istanbul's independent theatre life is split between two poles.

**Pera and Beyoğlu**, on the European side, is the historic theatre district: İstiklal Avenue, the reopened Muammer Karaca Theatre, and a cluster of small stages and cultural centres. It is central and easy to reach by metro, funicular and tram.

**Kadıköy**, on the Asian side, is where much of the city's independent scene now lives: small stages, a younger audience, bars and cafés that host open mics and language nights. It is easiest to reach by ferry, which is, honestly, the best commute in the city.

If you live on one side, start there. Crossing the Bosphorus every week for a workshop sounds romantic and is, after the third rainy Monday, a commitment.

## Why doing beats watching

A practical point to end on. Watching a play in a language you do not speak is, at best, an aesthetic experience: you follow the bodies, the light, the music. That can be wonderful. But it keeps you outside.

A drama workshop puts you inside. You work with the same group every week, in a shared language, on something that requires you to listen, react and trust people. That is also why drama classes are one of the most underrated ways to build a social life in a new city. We wrote about that separately in our guide to [making friends in Istanbul as an expat](/en/articles/make-friends-istanbul-expat-drama).

## FAQ

**Is there an English-language theatre company in Istanbul?**
We could not verify any English-language company with a regular public season in 2026. English-language performance in Istanbul happens mostly through comedy nights, festival productions partly in English, and drama workshops.

**Do Istanbul's City Theatres or State Theatres have English surtitles?**
We could not find an official statement from either institution about English surtitles. Check each production individually; do not assume.

**When is the Istanbul Theatre Festival in 2026?**
The 30th edition runs from 22 October to 3 December 2026. Surtitles on the production pages we checked are in Turkish; some productions are partly in English or non-verbal.

**Can I take acting or drama classes in English in Istanbul?**
Yes. Techne Lab's English Drama Lab runs weekly in English in Pera and Kadıköy, with free introduction sessions. Speech Bubbles runs English drama for young learners. Several language-exchange groups run informal improv and acting-game nights in English.

**Do I need acting experience?**
Not for drama workshops like ours. You need a working level of English (we ask for B1 and above) and the willingness to try things in front of other people.

**I am only in Istanbul for a few weeks. Can I still join?**
Our English Drama Lab can be joined for 4, 8 or 12 weeks, and the weekly introduction session is free. See our guide to [short theatre workshops in Istanbul](/en/articles/theatre-workshop-istanbul-short-stay).
