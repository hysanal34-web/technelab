Most guides to Istanbul are guides to looking. Look at the mosques, the Bosphorus, the cisterns, the sunset from a ferry. All of it is worth looking at.

But if you are staying longer than a weekend, as a digital nomad on a three-month stint, a student on exchange, someone working remotely from Kadıköy for a season, or a visitor who simply wants something other than another museum queue, a different question appears: how do you stop looking at the city and start being in it?

One answer, which we are biased towards because it is what we do, is a room full of people doing theatre together in English. This guide explains what that looks like in Istanbul, how short you can go, and how to choose.

## Why a drama workshop, of all things?

Because it solves three problems visitors have at once.

**You meet people, not just other visitors.** Expat meetups are full of other expats. Tours are full of other tourists. A drama workshop in English in Istanbul is open to both: Turkish participants who work or think in English and want to use it, and internationals who live here or are passing through. You end up improvising a scene with people whose paths would never otherwise cross yours. That is a different Istanbul.

**You do something, together, every week.** Friendships form through repeated, shared, slightly uncomfortable experiences. A weekly workshop provides exactly that. A one-off event rarely does.

**It works without Turkish.** Most of Istanbul's cultural life happens in Turkish. Drama in English is one of the few creative spaces you can walk into on your first week in the city and fully take part in.

## What exists in Istanbul (October 2026)

Here is what we could verify. We have put our own programmes first because we know them in detail, not because they are the only option.

### Free weekly introduction sessions

At Techne Lab we run free introduction sessions for **English Drama Lab**, our adult drama workshop in English, every week:

- **Pera (Beyoğlu)**: Saturdays at 15:00
- **Kadıköy**: Mondays at 20:00

If you are in the city for a short time, this is the simplest way in. You come once, you do a real session with the group, and you see whether it is for you. No commitment.

### Four-week formats

English Drama Lab can be joined for **12, 8 or 4 weeks**. The four-week option exists precisely for people whose time in Istanbul is limited. Groups are capped at twelve participants, and we ask for a working level of English (B1 and above). No acting experience is needed.

### Drop-in improv and acting games

Istanbul's language-exchange scene, mostly on Meetup, includes informal drop-in evenings. In October 2026, one English club lists a weekly "Act in English" night built around improv and acting games. These are social and loosely structured: a good option if you want a single evening rather than a course.

### Stand-up open mics

If what you want is a stage rather than a workshop, English stand-up open mics run in Kadıköy. TuzBiber Stand-Up, for example, lists an English Open Mic at Aylak Kadıköy on 11 October 2026.

### Children and teenagers

If you are in Istanbul with children aged 10 to 17, our **English Drama Youth** groups run weekly in Pera and Kadıköy, also with free introduction sessions. Speech Bubbles School of Performing Arts runs drama in English for young learners in Saturday terms.

## What actually happens in a session

If you have never done a drama workshop, here is roughly what to expect. This describes how we work; other groups will differ.

**Warm-up.** Walking, games, names, a few exercises to wake up the body and the group. Nobody is put on the spot. Everyone is doing the same thing at the same time.

**Improvisation.** Short scenes made up on the spot from a simple prompt: two people at a bus stop, a customer and a shop assistant, a family dinner where something goes wrong. The aim is not to be funny or clever; it is to listen and respond.

**Scene work.** Longer pieces, sometimes built by the group, sometimes from a short text. You try a scene, change one thing, and try it again.

**Reflection.** A short conversation at the end: what happened, what surprised you, what you noticed.

You do not need to memorise anything. You do not need to perform alone in front of everyone on your first day. You do need to be willing to try things and get them wrong in front of other people, which is the whole point.

## How short is too short?

Honestly: one session is a taste, four weeks is an experience, and anything longer starts to build something.

- **One session** gives you an evening of meeting people and doing something unusual. Worth it, if that is all the time you have.
- **Four weeks** is enough for a group to form. By the third week you know names, you have running jokes, and the work gets braver.
- **Eight to twelve weeks** is where you see real change: in confidence, in the quality of the scenes, and often in friendships that outlast the course.

If you are in Istanbul for around a month, the four-week format is designed for you.

## What to ask before you sign up (anywhere)

Whether you join us or someone else, these questions will save you a disappointing evening:

1. **What language is the workshop actually run in?** "International" or "multilingual" sometimes means Turkish with occasional English. Ask directly.
2. **What level of English do participants have?** A workshop where everyone is a beginner feels very different from one where people work in English daily.
3. **How big is the group?** Twelve people is a workshop. Thirty people is a show.
4. **Can I come once before committing?** A good group will let you try.
5. **Can I join for a short period?** Some programmes run only as full terms.
6. **Is there a final performance?** Some people love that; some people want to avoid it. Know which you are.
7. **Where exactly is it?** In Istanbul, "Kadıköy" or "Beyoğlu" can still mean a 40-minute walk. Check the address against where you are staying.

## Getting there

**Pera / Beyoğlu** is on the European side, near İstiklal Avenue. It is well connected by metro (Şişhane and Taksim stations), the Tünel funicular from Karaköy, and the nostalgic tram on İstiklal.

**Kadıköy** is on the Asian side. The ferry from Karaköy or Eminönü takes around twenty minutes and is one of the best things you can do in Istanbul for the price of a transport card. The Marmaray rail line also connects the two sides under the Bosphorus.

## FAQ

**Are there theatre workshops in English in Istanbul?**
Yes. Techne Lab's English Drama Lab runs weekly in English in Pera and Kadıköy. Informal drop-in improv nights in English also run through language-exchange groups.

**Can I join for just a few weeks?**
English Drama Lab can be joined for 4, 8 or 12 weeks. The weekly introduction session is free and needs no commitment.

**Do I need to speak Turkish?**
No. The workshop is run entirely in English.

**Do I need acting experience?**
No. You need a working level of English (B1 and above) and a willingness to try.

**Is it suitable for introverts?**
Often more than people expect. Everyone works at the same time in the early exercises, nobody is forced to perform alone, and the structure takes away the pressure of making small talk.

**Is the introduction session really free?**
Yes. Our weekly introduction sessions are free. Register through the [introduction session page](/tanisma-gunu?program=english-drama-lab) or write to us via [contact](/iletisim) in English.
